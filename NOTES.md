# Notes

## What the judge rewards, in order

The judge picks the text that leaves it more curious: alert, drawn in, wanting more. The criteria run in order, and the first real difference decides, unless a later one is a much wider gap.

1. Intrigue. The reading has to draw you in and leave you asking. A text that was not written to intrigue, or that leaves you more tense, loses to any text that intrigues without that tension.
2. Calm that is in the scene. It shows. It does not tell the reader to relax, to wonder, or to feel anything. Nothing in it feels forced.
3. Craft. Rhythm, pace, and sound that slow the reading. Plain, concrete, gentle words. Any word that can go, goes.
4. Linger. One image or thought you can come back to, still working after the last line.
5. Freshness. Its own voice and pictures. Stock wellness lines and a meditation script in new clothes both lose.

Length by itself does not help. Two texts are almost never equal.

## What the strongest rivals will do

The sister contests rewarded a tiny physical scene: short lines, ordinary objects, one quiet turn, no orders to the reader. Rivals here will keep that shape and try to bend it toward curiosity.

The weak majority will miss the brief. Some will add dread: a bolt drawn, a wet print, a radio that was off. Those lose on the first criterion, because tension loses to calm intrigue. Some will ask the reader a riddle, or say "notice" and "wonder," which fails the second criterion by instructing. Some will speak to the model, flatter it, or ask it to choose them. Some will retell the awe scenes (sleep, boats, a vast soft dark) with a question stuck on the end. That feeling settles. It does not keep asking.

The serious cluster will be a calm habit in an ordinary place, plus one unexplained detail. Many of those will still close: the detail will turn out to be kindness with a nameable meaning, or a clue with a solution. Both let the reader put the text down.

## The angle that beats them

A doorstep milk book is as ordinary as a place gets. A second, smaller hand has added a standard: the heel, and only while it is warm. She meets the standard. By morning the plate is back on the rack, the milk bottle is full of water, and under the cap is a sprig of thyme, the cut end still damp.

There is no one to fear and nothing to solve. The water is the wrong thing in the right bottle. The stem was cut recently enough to stay damp, so the exchange is still going, and the small letters may be in the book again tomorrow. The picture you return to is the cut end under the cap.

## Tournament

Sixteen scenes, all different angles. Judged in the brief's order.

Out on the first pass: the cellar bolt and the saucer ring leave a chill; the bus ticket grieves and turns the punches into a code; the gate thread asks the reader the question out loud; the pocket stones arrange a ritual and a trick drawer; the jay explains itself as a clever bird; the bakery shoes vanish just before he arrives; the greenhouse stone is only "who moved it"; the library pencil has no handle to keep turning; the hall clock collapses into a housemate who likes it fast; the orchard chair is a finished gesture of care, nearer awe; the two cups are the same shape as the dairy book with the answer (someone ate) already in the crumb.

Best four:

- Dairy book and thyme. A voice with a standard, a bottle of water, a damp cut stem.
- Seed packet. A bean he does not grow, a second hole a palm away, a stick rubbed smooth.
- Nail tin. A brass screw he does not stock, the nail back by noon, red paint in the thread.
- Footbridge coin. The same nicked coin, the nick turned toward the water.

The nail loses to the dairy book on intrigue. The paint in the thread is a clue, and a clue wants a solution. The thyme has no solution hiding in it, so the questions stay open: who writes so small, why only a warm heel, why water, whose plant is still damp.

The coin loses on intrigue as well. The nick toward the water is a still picture. It is calm and well made, and it asks less. It sits nearer quiet awe.

The seed packet is the close one. It also opens forward: an unknown bean, a polite distance of a palm, a stick finished so it will not catch a hand, a sprout still ahead. The dairy book is more alert. Someone has already spoken, in six words, and the stem is damp now, not next week. That is the first difference, on intrigue. Craft is close between them. The packet does not pull ahead far enough on linger or freshness to overturn it.

Winner: the dairy book. 278 code points.
