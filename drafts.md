# Drafts

Counts are Unicode code points.

## 1. Dairy book (278)

She writes "one pint" in the book by the door.
Under it, smaller letters: "the heel, if still warm."
She sets the warm heel on the step.
By morning the plate is back on the rack.
The milk bottle is full of water.
A sprig of thyme is tucked under the cap,
the cut end still damp.

## 2. Seed packet (270)

An unmarked packet lies at the front of the seed drawer.
The beans are mottled, a kind he does not grow.
He plants one at the end of the row.
By morning a second hole sits a palm to the left.
The packet is one bean lighter.
A stick marks the hole, its top rubbed smooth.

## 3. Nail tin (255)

A brass screw stands in the nail tin by the shop door.
He does not stock that screw.
Yesterday the tin held only nails.
The longest nail is gone.
He leaves the tin.
By noon the nail is back, bright at the tip,
and red paint sits in one turn of the thread.

## 4. Footbridge coin (258)

He pays the footbridge with a nicked coin and takes the ticket.
By evening the ticket is back in the slot.
The same coin lies on the lid, heads up.
He spends the other coins.
Next morning the nicked coin is on the lid again,
the nick turned toward the water.

## 5. Orchard chair (258)

A wooden chair stands at the end of the orchard row.
Morning, it faces the trees.
Dusk, it faces the house.
A green leaf is tucked under the front slat, stem first.
The branch above it is one leaf thinner.
The wind lifts the grass. It does not lift the leaf.

## 6. Hall clock (259)

The hall clock loses a minute a day.
He sets it each Sunday and leaves the key in the hole.
On Wednesday the key hangs on the other hook.
The hands stand three minutes ahead of his.
He does not move them.
Sunday, the key is back, and the three minutes remain.

## 7. Two cups (243)

The night cook sets two cups on the pass.
He drinks from the blue one.
He turns the white one a quarter turn when the bread comes out.
By morning both cups are clean.
The white one has been turned back.
One warm crumb sits in the bottom of it.

## 8. Greenhouse stone (207)

A flat stone covers the stem in the end pot.
She moves it to the dry pot beside that one.
In the morning the stone is back on the first pot.
The dry soil is dark, a finger deep.
The top of the stone is warm.

## 9. Library pencil (261)

A pencil lies on the library ledge, its point toward the street.
At noon the point is blunt and turned toward the room.
By closing it faces the street again, and it is sharp.
One curl of shaving sits where a hand will find it.
The curl was not there at opening.

## 10. Bakery shoes (266)

At dawn a second pair of shoes stands on the bakery step,
flour on one heel, toes toward the lane.
He never arrives in time to meet them.
The trays inside are turned, pale rolls to the oven's warm side.
A floury thumbprint on the prover door sits level with his own.

## 11. Jay (250)

A jay lifts one seed from the feeder and sets it on the rail.
It splits the shell, then sets the kernel back in the halves.
The kernel stays when the bird goes.
It is still balanced when she opens the door.
The shell rocks once in the wind and holds.

## 12. Saucer (258)

She sets a saucer of water on the step at dusk.
By morning the water is gone.
A wet ring the size of a coin sits in the dust beside the saucer.
No prints lead to it or away.
She sets the next saucer down on the ring.
By morning the ring is on the other side.

## 13. Pocket stones (265)

Each Monday a small stone waits in her coat pocket.
She sets it on the sill. By evening the sill is bare.
The next stone has a white band across it.
She keeps a drawer for them.
The drawer holds only the newest stone.
The others are on the path again, set point up.

## 14. Bus ticket (250)

An old ticket from a cancelled route lies on the third-row sill.
Each morning it has a new punch, and the edges are softer.
The driver does not take it.
When the bus turns east the sun drops a dot of light
through the newest hole onto the empty seat.

## 15. Gate thread (220)

A red thread is tied to the gate each Friday morning.
Who ties it, and who cuts it down by Monday?
The cut end is tucked under the latch,
so the wind will not pull the knot loose.
The thread is a different red each week.

## 16. Cellar bolt (205)

The cellar door is bolted from the inside.
Each morning the bolt is open.
One wet bootprint dries on the stone,
pointing out toward the lane,
too large to be hers,
and the bolt is wet where a hand drew it.

## 17. Extra cane (187)

Past the last of her canes,
one more stands in the soil.
A bean leaf at its foot
is still folded.
Dry grit lies in the crease.
The cane is tied in the knot she uses,
the short end tucked.

## 18. Extra cane, knot first (175)

Past the last of her canes,
one more stands in the soil,
tied in the knot she uses,
the short end tucked.
A bean leaf at its foot
is still folded.
Dry grit lies in the crease.

## 19. Shell in the path (149)

A new stone is set in the path by the gate.
The concrete is pale, and soft at the edge.
A shell is pressed into it,
hinge up,
the ridges still sharp.

## 20. Cleat (136)

The line on his cleat is coiled in his knot.
One turn sits above it,
a turn he does not use.
That turn is wet.
The coil under it is dry.

## 21. Dairy, cut (220)

She writes "one pint" in the book by the door.
Under it, smaller: "the heel, if still warm."
She sets the heel on the step.
By morning the bottle is back.
A sprig of thyme is tucked under the cap,
the cut end still damp.

## 22. Heel and water (147)

She sets the warm heel on the step.
By morning the bottle is back,
full of water.
A sprig of thyme is tucked under the cap,
the cut end still damp.

## 23. Blue wool (169)

The book comes back to the desk
with a strand of blue wool between the pages.
It marks a later page than last week.
The end is newly cut,
square, the fibers still loose.

## 24. Sunday mint (159)

A peppermint waits in the pocket of her Sunday robe.
The wrapper's ends are twisted the same way as last week.
This one is a different mint,
in the same twist.

## Mini-tournament

Played the dairy book against the brave scenes, the awe scenes, and the habit-and-detail cluster. The brave scenes complete an action and then rest. The awe scenes slow down and settle. Neither keeps asking. Against those, the dairy book wins on intrigue and loses on shape: too many objects, a reveal before the last line, and a plate that was never set down.

Within the new set: the mint closes into a shy friend. The wool summarizes a week and turns the strand into a clue. The cleat proves its wet turn, which is a small puzzle. The shell asks mostly where a shell came from, and stays still. Water in the bottle is the strangest fact and the uneasy one. The dairy book with the plate and the water cut is calmer than v1, and still a quoted rule plus a doorstep gift. Putting the knot before the leaf makes the leaf into evidence.

The extra cane wins. One cane past hers, a leaf not yet open, dry grit in the crease, and only then her knot, the short end tucked. The grit means it has already been there. The leaf is still ahead. The turn is the last line.
